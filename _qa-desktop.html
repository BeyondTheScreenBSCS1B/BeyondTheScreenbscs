01 — About the campaign

More than a poster.
A habit of checking.

Technology is woven into school, friendships and downtime. Our campaign builds the awareness — and the reflexes — to navigate it safely, responsibly and with a critical eye.

Beyond The Screen: Think Before You Click is a campaign aimed at raising awareness around digital literacy, tech responsibility and critical thinking. With artificial intelligence developing quickly, it has become increasingly important to understand what is going on behind the screen.

We help learners tell the difference between content created by humans and AI, recognise misinformation and deceptive digital content, and understand both the benefits and the potential risks of new technology.

Through education, awareness and responsible use, we want to sharpen your skills and make you a smarter digital citizen. Technology should be used responsibly — we shouldn't take information for granted, and we should be mindful when clicking.

Prepared by six Lasallian BSCS & BSIT studentsSTS Project · 2026

Falling for fake news?

Not everything online is true. Check the source, the date, and whether other trusted sites report the same story.

Too good to be true?

Free prizes, urgent messages and "limited time offers" are classic scam tricks. We show you the red flags before you click.

Not sure what to share?

One share can spread a lie fast. We promote pause, verify, then post — so you help stop misinformation instead of spreading it.

02 — Needs assessment

We started by
listening.

Before creating solutions, we needed to understand the experiences, challenges and digital habits of our target learners.

Our target community

Senior High School students

Our needs assessment focused on Senior High School learners to understand their access to technology, digital skills, online experiences and challenges.

"Technology is everywhere — but are students using it safely?"
45 respondents Needs survey SHS learners
The identified problem

Although Senior High School students regularly use digital technology for education, communication and entertainment, they continue to experience challenges with online safety, internet accessibility, digital well-being and responsible technology use.

Many have encountered online safety concerns, struggle with unstable connections, and find it difficult to balance technology with daily routines. These findings highlight the importance of building digital literacy, cybersecurity awareness, and responsible use of emerging technologies such as artificial intelligence.

Our priority

Building smarter and safer digital citizens

Strengthen students' digital literacy and promote safe, responsible and effective technology use — especially in cybersecurity, identifying online threats, responsible AI usage and digital well-being.

05 — Evaluation

Did it
land?

At the end of the session we gave participants a short Google Forms evaluation — about three minutes — to hear what worked and what we can improve.

The instrument

A three-minute check-in

  • Basic information: topic, date and audience group.
  • Statements rated on a 5-point scale — 5 strongly agree, 1 strongly disagree.
  • Three themes: content, delivery and impact.
  • Reflection questions, optional feedback and a personal pledge.

Google Forms evaluation · Expo day, November 18, 2026

Content

Was the topic clear, useful, relevant and complete in covering scams, red flags, AI-made pictures and checking the source?

Delivery

Were the presenters clear and engaging? Were the "Scam or Safe?" and "Real or AI?" activities, the slides and the pacing effective?

Impact

Did participants learn something new, spot a scam red flag and know how to verify a picture's origin? Can this help the school and community?

Key insights

The session made participants more aware and careful online. Many admitted they used to click or share posts without checking, and now see how easily fake news spreads. They learned that scammers want people to rush or panic — so messages like "act now or lose your account" are a red flag. Some said anyone can be scammed, even people who are good with technology. Others want to teach their families what they learned.

Their main takeaway Think before you click.

the Global Goals.

The problem we address

Access alone isn't enough.

As technology becomes increasingly integrated into students' daily lives, many still face challenges identifying misinformation, protecting personal information, recognising online threats, and using artificial intelligence responsibly.

Our project addresses the need to develop responsible digital habits and equip students with the knowledge to navigate the online world safely and wisely.

Digital Awareness

Better understanding of AI, misinformation and digital literacy.

Online Safety

Improved cybersecurity awareness and personal information protection.

Community Impact

Encouraging responsible technology use among students and peers.

Our contribution to a better digital future

We hope to build a community where students do not simply use technology but understand their responsibilities when using it. By promoting digital literacy, cybersecurity awareness and responsible innovation, we aim to contribute to a generation that is more informed, capable and prepared for the challenges of the digital world.
